# Decisions

Resolutions to the `> OPEN:` items in this spec that materially affect the database schema, security and privacy, tenant-versus-landlord behaviour, navigation, payment and rent logic, maintenance behaviour, receipts, tokens, or documents. Each decision records the conflicting source requirements, why they cannot both be implemented literally, the product decision, and its consequences.

Decisions are numbered `D-nn`. A screen file's OPEN line is resolved when a decision here cites it. OPEN items that are purely visual or wording-level and do not touch any of the areas above are listed at the end as intentionally remaining.

Element IDs marked † are assigned here, in the same way the conversion assigned IDs the source lacked.

## 0 · Precedence

### D-00 · Which source wins when the two documents disagree

**Conflict.** The product scope document (product.md) and the UI/UX specification disagree in several places: a month-against-unit grid vs a single-month table (M-2 vs L-04), "three places only" for the assistant vs five S-01 entry points (§11 vs S-01), wireframe orderings vs L-03's rules, "no path to any money screen" vs T-05.

**Why not both.** The two documents describe different products in those places. The UI/UX spec's own cover says "treat as the source of truth for version one".

**Decision.** The UI/UX specification governs wherever it speaks. product.md governs only where the UI/UX spec is silent (the ERD, the rent lifecycle's day counts, the connection list). Where product.md names a behaviour that no UI/UX screen implements, that behaviour is not in v1 unless a decision below adds it.

**Consequences.** Resolves: product.md M-2 grid OPEN, L-04 wireframe-B OPEN, L-03 wireframe-A OPEN, product.md §7 OPENs. The wireframes in product.md §7 are illustrations, not requirements. No grid view of rent in v1.

## 1 · Tokens and tenant access

### D-01 · Two tokens: `unit.door_token` and `tenancy.tenant_token` (mandatory schema correction 1)

**Conflict.** T-01 route: "one unguessable token per unit". T-01 states: "A former tenant's link stops working when the tenancy ends"; E4-2 tests exactly that. product.md §4: "Every tenant gets their own unit link". product.md §15: UNIT has "its own reporting link". P-03 prints a QR for a door sticker that outlives tenancies. T-05 shows receipts, which belong to one person.

**Why not both.** A single per-unit token cannot stop working for the former tenant while continuing to work for the new one. A single per-tenancy token cannot be printed on a door sticker that stays valid across tenancies.

**Decision.** Two tokens, both long random opaque strings, both served at `/u/:token`:

- `unit.door_token` — created with the unit, never expires on its own, printed on P-03 and shown by L09-BTN-QR. It can be regenerated by the landlord (invalidates printed stickers; this is the only way to revoke it).
- `tenancy.tenant_token` — created with the tenancy, revoked when the tenancy ends (D-05). This is "the tenant's link": what L09-BTN-COPYLINK / L09-BTN-SHARELINK copy, what the welcome share (C-8) sends, and what T-02 asks the tenant to save when they arrived by it.

Token lookup resolves to exactly one of the two; the route handler never accepts unit ids, tenancy ids or unit numbers. Tokens are stored in clear, not hashed: they are bearer URLs the landlord must be able to copy again from L-09. They are unique indexed columns with at least 128 bits of entropy.

**Consequences.** Resolves: T-01 token OPEN, product.md §4 OPEN, index.md phase-2 "tokens generated", P-03 which-link OPEN, L-09 which-link OPEN, cross-cutting E4-2 OPEN, T-02 "Save this link" OPEN. Phase 2 "tokens generated" means both. What a door token may reach is D-02. `/u/:token` for a vacant unit's door token shows the invalid-link message (T-01 "Unit is vacant" state) — a door token never has a current tenancy to attach a report to when vacant.

### D-02 · What the door token can reach

**Conflict.** T-01 is "the entry point" and P-03's QR exists so anyone in the flat can report a problem. T-03, T-04 and T-05 show the tenancy's reports, agreement dates and receipts with amounts. A door sticker can be photographed by anyone who enters the flat (a vendor, a viewer, a former tenant).

**Why not both.** The door token cannot be revoked per person, so anything it exposes is exposed to everyone who has ever seen the sticker.

**Decision.** The door token reaches **T-01 and T-02 only**. A report made through it attaches to the unit's current tenancy (D-29). Under a door token: T01-FLD-NAME and T01-FLD-PHONE are blank, not pre-filled (the tenant's name and phone are not shown to whoever scanned the sticker); T01-LNK-MYREPORTS and the footer links (D-03) are not rendered; T02-BTN-TRACK is replaced by a one-line note that status is shown on the link the landlord sent (wording belongs to E1); T02-BTN-SAVE still offers the door link, which is enough to report again. T-03, T-04, T-05 and the tenant P-01 route return the invalid-link message for a door token. The tenant's personal link (`tenant_token`) reaches all five pages.

**Consequences.** Resolves: P-03 which-link OPEN (the sticker is report-only by design, so it can outlive tenancies). The tenant server resolves a door token to `{landlord_id, unit_id, tenancy_id}` for creating a request and nothing else; it never reads payments or agreements for a door token. New boundary test `E4-8` †: "Open any route other than T-01/T-02 with a door token → Invalid link message." The landlord is told on L-09 (helper text under L09-BTN-QR †) that the door code is for reporting only and the tenant's own link is the one to share.

### D-03 · Tenant routes and "the tenant's own three pages"

**Conflict.** A3: tenant screens link only to "the tenant's own three pages". The spec has five tenant screens (T-01…T-05); product.md §8 says "four tenant pages". T-02, T-03, T-04 and T-05 have no routes. T-04 and T-05 have no entry point at all. P-01 opened from T-05 must be reachable without sign-in.

**Why not both.** Screens with no route or entry point cannot be built; "three pages" cannot literally be five.

**Decision.** Routes, all under the token so no other identifier is ever in a tenant URL:

| Screen | Route |
|---|---|
| T-01 | `/u/:token` |
| T-02 | `/u/:token/reported/:requestNumber` |
| T-03 | `/u/:token/reports` |
| T-04 | `/u/:token/unit` |
| T-05 | `/u/:token/receipts` |
| P-01 (tenant) | `/u/:token/receipts/:receiptNumber/print` |

"The tenant's own three pages" are T-03, T-04 and T-05. T-01 is the entry point and T-02 is the transient confirmation. T-01 gains a footer row of three links † (`T01-LNK-MYUNIT`, `T01-LNK-RECEIPTS` alongside the existing T01-LNK-MYREPORTS); T-03, T-04 and T-05 each carry the same three links plus "Report a problem" → T-01. Nothing else is linked, per the Part C brief.

P-01 for the landlord is `/print/receipt/:paymentId` (signed in). Both routes render the same template (D-26).

**Consequences.** Resolves: foundations A3 "three pages" OPEN, T-02 route OPEN, T-04 entry-point OPEN, T-05 entry-point OPEN, P-01 route OPEN. Request numbers and receipt numbers are per landlord (D-12), so they are unique within a token. T-02 reopened after a refresh re-renders from the request number; it shows the invalid-link message if the request does not belong to the token's tenancy.

### D-04 · The permission boundary, restated for two tokens

**Conflict.** E4-1 "Open a unit link belonging to another landlord's unit → Invalid link message" cannot be made to fail: a valid link is valid for its own unit. E4-5/E4-7 require "Not found" across landlords while signed in.

**Why not both.** A test that cannot fail is not a test.

**Decision.** Every table carries `landlord_id`. Row-level security on the landlord side filters by the signed-in landlord's id (E4-5, E4-7). The tenant side runs through `src/server/tenant` only, which resolves a token to `{landlord_id, unit_id, tenancy_id | null}` and selects only the tenant-safe columns listed in D-26 and D-30; it never joins to other units or tenancies. E4-1 is restated as: "Take a valid token from landlord A's unit and use it with any identifier belonging to landlord B (request number, receipt number, unit id in any form) → Invalid link message." E4-3 (altered token) and E4-2 (ended tenancy, D-05) stand as written.

**Consequences.** Resolves: cross-cutting E4-1 OPEN. There are no tenant-facing endpoints that take a unit id or tenancy id; D-03's routes are the complete list. The seed's "second landlord for E4 cross-landlord tests" becomes required for the restated E4-1.

### D-05 · Ending a tenancy: End tenancy, Mark settled, and token revocation

**Conflict.** L11-BTN-ENDTENANCY "→ confirm dialog → routes to L-13". L13-BTN-CLOSE "Mark settled → tenancy closes, unit becomes vacant". T-01 "A former tenant's link stops working when the tenancy ends". L09-BTN-ADDTENANT "Visible only when vacant". A new tenant may move in before the old deposit is settled.

**Why not both.** Two different buttons each claim to close the tenancy; the unit cannot become vacant at both moments.

**Decision.** `tenancy.status` is `active | ended | settled`.

- **End tenancy** (L-11) asks for the move-out date in its confirm dialog (defaults to today) and sets `ended_on` and `status = ended`. From this moment: `tenant_token` is revoked (E4-2), no further rent rows are created, the unit has no active tenancy and so shows Vacant on L-08/L-09, and L09-BTN-ADDTENANT appears.
- **Mark settled** (L-13) sets `status = settled` and files the protected statement (D-15). If the landlord reached L-13 for a still-active tenancy (via `/deposits/:tenantId`), Mark settled performs End tenancy first with today's date.

**Consequences.** Resolves: L-11 End-tenancy OPEN, cross-cutting E4-2 OPEN (with D-01). "Unit becomes vacant" in L-13 is already true by then. L-10's "Past" filter = `ended` or `settled`. L13-TBL-HELD lists tenancies that are `active` or `ended` (deposit still held); settled ones leave it. A former tenant who needs a receipt after move-out asks the landlord, who prints or shares it from L-04/L-09.

### D-06 · The welcome message with the unit link

**Conflict.** product.md §5 stage 2: the tenant "Gets a welcome message with their unit link"; C-3 lists "welcoming a new tenant with their link"; C-11 lists "the welcome sheet". No UI/UX screen opens S-01 with a welcome draft or defines a welcome sheet.

**Why not both.** Nothing in the UI/UX spec produces the message.

**Decision.** In v1 the welcome is the landlord sharing the tenant link with L09-BTN-SHARELINK or L09-BTN-COPYLINK (C-8 "sending a new tenant their unit link"). No welcome draft, no welcome sheet. The door QR (P-03) is the printed companion.

**Consequences.** Resolves: product.md §5 stage-2 welcome OPEN; the C-3 and C-11 welcome entries in the §12 connections OPEN are closed as "not a screen in v1". No new S-01 entry point.

## 2 · Core entities

### D-07 · `tenancy` is the ERD's TENANT; there is no separate person table

**Conflict.** product.md §15 TENANT holds name, phone, agreement dates, deposit paid and notice status in one entity. Routes use `/tenants/:tenantId` and `/deposits/:tenantId`. L-10 lists "past or present" tenants. The mandatory corrections place `tenant_token` and notice on a *tenancy*.

**Why not both.** The corrections require an entity per occupation of a unit; the ERD has one entity per person-in-unit. They are the same thing under two names.

**Decision.** One table, `tenancy` = one person occupying one unit for one continuous period. It carries the person's `name`, `phone`, `email` (optional), `moved_in_on`, `ended_on`, `status` (D-05), `deposit_paid`, `deposit_received_on`, notice fields (D-13) and `tenant_token` (D-01). `:tenantId` in every route is a tenancy id. A person who rents twice has two tenancies; v1 does not link them.

**Consequences.** Resolves: P-02 "tenancy dates" OPEN (they are `moved_in_on`/`ended_on`, with agreement terms listed from D-08). L-10/L-11 "a person" means a tenancy. The seed's 13 "tenancies" map directly.

### D-08 · `agreement` is its own entity (mandatory schema correction 3)

**Conflict.** product.md §15 puts "agreement start, end" on TENANT. L12-BTN-RENEW: "On save the agreement dates update … future rent rows use the new amount"; "Rows already generated for earlier months are not altered." L-11 shows rent and deposit per agreement. L-09 Rent tab: "every month, every tenant, ever", with the amount that applied. P-02 needs the terms. The seed carries three terms for one tenancy.

**Why not both.** Overwriting dates on the tenancy destroys the history the other screens display.

**Decision.** `agreement` rows belong to a tenancy: `starts_on`, `ends_on`, `rent`, `rent_due_day` (D-09), `escalation_pct_applied` (nullable), `created_at`. A tenancy has one or more, non-overlapping, ordered by `starts_on`. "Record renewal" inserts a new agreement; it never edits the old one. The agreement "in force" on a date is the one whose range contains it; if none does and the date is after the last `ends_on`, the last agreement is in force at its rent (L-12: an expired agreement "stays there until renewed or the tenancy is ended").

L-12 lists each tenancy's latest agreement. After renewal the row "leaves the list" because the latest agreement's `ends_on` is now outside the window; under "All" it reappears with the new end date. L11-SEC-AGREEMENT shows the current agreement and lists earlier terms beneath it †.

**Consequences.** Resolves: product.md §15 agreement OPEN, L-12 overwrite OPEN, L-12 "row leaves the list" OPEN, L-12 expired-continues OPEN, L-11 single-set OPEN. The renewal draft's effective date is the new agreement's `starts_on`, which defaults to the day after the current `ends_on` in L12-BTN-RENEW's form. Suggested new rent = current rent × (1 + escalation %) rounded to the whole rupee (D-19).

### D-09 · `agreement.rent_due_day` and how days overdue are counted (mandatory schema correction 4)

**Conflict.** product.md §15 RENT ENTRY has a "due date" but no entity carries a due day. product.md §10 starts at "1st of the month", then asks "Paid by the due date?", then "Day 3 · gentle nudge". L-15 makes the ladder days editable. L04-CHP-STATUS shows "n days late".

**Why not both.** "Day 3" cannot be the 3rd of the month if the due date is the 5th. Without a stored due day no row can have a due date.

**Decision.** `agreement.rent_due_day` (1–31) is set when the agreement is created and may differ per renewal. A rent entry's `due_on` = that day in its month, clamped to the month's last day. "Days late" = today − `due_on`, counted from the due date, not the 1st. Ladder days (L15-TBL-LADDER, default 3 / 10 / 20) are days after `due_on`. L04-STRIP/L-03 "past due" means today > `due_on`.

**Consequences.** Resolves: product.md §15 due-day OPEN, L-04 due-date OPEN, product.md §10 day-count OPEN. The L-02 unit table and L-08 "Add unit" form do not collect a due day; the tenant form (D-39) does, defaulting to the 1st.

### D-10 · `payment` is a child of `rent_entry` (mandatory schema correction 2)

**Conflict.** product.md §15 RENT ENTRY holds a single "amount paid · paid on · payment reference · receipt number". L-04: part payment "remains in the Pending filter. A receipt is issued for the amount actually received." L-04: "Marking paid twice is impossible — the button is replaced the moment the state changes." L05-SEC-AMOUNT shows a single paid amount. product.md §14 says one row per unit per month.

**Why not both.** One set of payment fields cannot hold two instalments, and a second instalment needs its own receipt.

**Decision.** `rent_entry` = one row per tenancy per month: `tenancy_id`, `unit_id`, `agreement_id`, `month`, `amount_due`, `due_on`, `is_pro_rata`. `payment` = one row per amount received: `rent_entry_id`, `amount`, `received_on`, `reference` (optional), `receipt_number` (D-12), `status: recorded | voided` (D-11). Derived state of a rent entry, from the sum of recorded payments:

| Derived status | Condition | L04-CHP-STATUS | L04-SEG-FILTER |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid | paid ≥ due | Paid | Paid |
| Due | paid < due, today ≤ due_on | Due (+ "Part paid — ₹n pending" sub-line when paid > 0) | Pending |
| Overdue | paid < due, today > due_on | n days late (+ the same sub-line) | Overdue |

"Pending" and "Overdue" are disjoint; a part-paid row sits in whichever its due date puts it. "Unpaid" in L-03's rules means Paid is not reached, so part-paid overdue rows appear. "Mark paid" stays available while the status is not Paid; what becomes impossible is recording more than the balance — L04-FRM-MARKPAID pre-fills the *balance* and caps at it. L05-SEC-AMOUNT lists every recorded payment with its receipt number. S02-FLD-AMOUNT pre-fills the balance. L-09 Rent tab shows paid total per month.

**Consequences.** Resolves: product.md §15 part-payment OPEN, L-04 part-payment OPEN, L-04 chip/filter mapping OPEN, L-05 single-amount OPEN, L-05 mark-paid OPEN (same inline form as L-04), S-02 balance OPEN, L-03 part-paid OPEN, L-09 part-payment OPEN. Receipts are rendered from `payment` rows on demand; they are not stored as `document` rows.

### D-11 · Undo of a recorded payment

**Conflict.** L-04: toast "with an Undo that reverses it for 10 seconds"; A2: toasts last 4 seconds. A receipt number has already been issued and may have been seen. Receipt sequences should be gapless for an accountant.

**Why not both.** Deleting the payment either reuses the number (two receipts with one number over time) or leaves a gap.

**Decision.** Undo sets `payment.status = voided` and writes an `audit_event` (D-14). Receipt numbers are never reused; a voided receipt keeps its number and is listed nowhere a tenant can see (T-05, P-01) and nowhere that totals money. L-05 shows voided payments struck through in its payment list so the gap is explained. The Undo toast for this action stays for 10 seconds; it is the one exception to the 4-second rule and is noted in A2.

**Consequences.** Resolves: L-04 undo-duration OPEN, L-04 receipt-on-undo OPEN, P-01 undo OPEN, T-05 voided OPEN, foundations toast-10s OPEN. After the 10 seconds the payment can only be corrected by voiding it from L-05 † (`L05-BTN-VOIDPAYMENT`, Danger, confirm dialog) and recording again.

### D-12 · Receipt and request numbering

**Conflict.** E1 shows "RR-0847" and "R-0412". Scope is not stated. A global sequence lets any tenant infer how many receipts or requests exist across all landlords.

**Why not both.** —

**Decision.** Both sequences are per landlord, allocated at insert, gapless at allocation, never reused: `receipt_number` = `RR-` + zero-padded to at least 4 digits; `request_number` = `R-` + the same. They are user-facing identifiers, not the "internal identifiers" E1's wording rule forbids.

**Consequences.** Resolves: L-07 request-number OPEN, P-01 numbering OPEN, cross-cutting internal-identifier OPEN. Numbers are unique per landlord, which is what D-03's tenant routes rely on.

### D-13 · Notice belongs on `tenancy` (mandatory schema correction 5)

**Conflict.** product.md §15 TENANT has "notice status". L08-CHP-STATUS has "Notice period"; L10-SEG-FILTER has "On notice"; L11-SEC-AGREEMENT shows "notice status". product.md M-7 says the landlord can "record a notice served"; journey stage 5 says the tenant "Confirms renewal, or gives notice". No screen sets notice.

**Why not both.** Displays with no writer are dead; the tenant pages have no form for notice and adding one would be new tenant-side behaviour.

**Decision.** `tenancy.notice_given_on` and `tenancy.notice_vacate_on` (both nullable). "On notice" = active tenancy with `notice_vacate_on` set. The tenant gives notice outside the app (call, WhatsApp); the landlord records it with `L11-BTN-RECORDNOTICE` † on L-11 (a two-field inline form: notice given on, vacating on; defaults today and today + 30 days; the 30 is a form default, not a stored rule). Recording notice writes an `audit_event`. L-08 chip "Notice period" and L-10 "On notice" derive from it. L-12 does not change: notice does not shorten the agreement; End tenancy (D-05) is still the act that ends the tenancy.

**Consequences.** Resolves: product.md §15 notice OPEN, product.md M-7 OPEN, product.md §5 stage-5 OPEN, L-08 notice OPEN, L-10 notice OPEN, L-11 notice OPEN, L-12 notice OPEN. C-9 "the notice period ending" calendar entry remains not in v1 (no element).

### D-14 · `audit_event` is its own entity (mandatory schema correction 6)

**Conflict.** L05-BTN-EDIT: "Any edit is recorded on the timeline with the old and new value." L-06: drag "writes a timeline entry"; reopening "writes a timeline entry". L07-TML-HISTORY: "Every status change, message sent and note added". L-13: "Corrections require a new statement that references the first". product.md §15 has no entity for any of this.

**Why not both.** Timelines cannot be rendered from data that is not stored.

**Decision.** `audit_event`: `landlord_id`, `actor` (`landlord | tenant | system`), `entity_type`, `entity_id`, `kind`, `old_value` and `new_value` (JSON, nullable), `note` (nullable), `created_at`. Written on: rent amount edit; payment recorded / voided; request created, status changed, reopened, vendor set, cost recorded, after-photo added, internal note added (`L07-BTN-ADDNOTE` †, a one-field form; notes are `audit_event` rows with `kind = note`); notice recorded; tenancy ended / settled; settlement superseded; document deleted; door token regenerated. Timelines: L07-TML-HISTORY = the request's audit events plus its confirmed messages (D-17); L05-TML-REMINDERS = the rent entry's confirmed messages and calls plus its amount-edit events.

**Consequences.** Resolves: product.md §15 audit OPEN, L-07 internal-notes OPEN. Audit rows are landlord-only; the tenant side (D-30) reads status-change events only, by `kind`, to build T03-TML-ITEM.

### D-15 · `document.is_protected`, document ownership and access (mandatory schema correction 7)

**Conflict.** L-13: once settled "the statement becomes read-only … filed under documents". L14-TBL-DOCS offers delete on every file. product.md §15 DOCUMENT "belongs to" a UNIT only, but photos belong to requests, tenancies (move-in), deductions, and the logo belongs to the landlord. S-04 does not say who may read a photo.

**Why not both.** A deletable file is not read-only; a unit-only parent cannot express the other owners.

**Decision.** `document`: `landlord_id`, `unit_id` (nullable — null only for the logo), `tenancy_id`, `request_id`, `settlement_id`, `deduction_id` (all nullable), `type` (`agreement | id_proof | move_in_photo | move_out_photo | request_before_photo | request_after_photo | deduction_photo | statement | logo`), `storage_path`, `file_name`, `mime`, `size_bytes`, `alt_text`, `is_protected` (default false), `uploaded_at`, `uploaded_by` (`landlord | tenant`). Files live in a private bucket and are served by short-lived signed URLs from the server only.

`is_protected = true` is set on the settlement statement at Mark settled. L-14's delete is disabled with a tooltip ("Settled statements cannot be deleted") for protected rows; the server refuses the delete regardless. Delete is a Danger action behind a confirm dialog. Nothing else sets `is_protected` in v1.

Tenant pages never fetch document files. T-02's summary and T-03 show photo *counts* ("2 photos attached"), not thumbnails; this keeps the tenant surface free of signed URLs and of any landlord-added after-photo.

**Consequences.** Resolves: product.md §15 protected OPEN, L-13 read-only OPEN, L-14 delete OPEN, L-14 button-variant OPEN, S-04 who-can-read OPEN. Receipts are not `document` rows (D-10). L-14 has no upload control (upload happens on L-07 after-photos, L-09 Photos & documents tab †, L-11 docs †, L-13 deductions, L-15 logo; each uses S-04 or a file picker). L15-UPL-LOGO accepts one image, replacing the previous.

### D-16 · `vendor` is its own entity

**Conflict.** L07-FLD-VENDOR is "a single text field" for "name and phone" that "remembers previously used vendors as suggestions". L07-BTN-DISPATCH sends to "the vendor's number". product.md §15 has no VENDOR; product.md §14 lists "Maintenance requests and vendors" as stored.

**Why not both.** A free-text field cannot reliably yield a phone number for the WhatsApp recipient, nor a stable "remembered" list.

**Decision.** `vendor`: `landlord_id`, `name`, `phone`, `created_at`. `request.vendor_id` (nullable). L07-FLD-VENDOR becomes a combobox: typing filters the landlord's vendors; "Add <name>" creates one and asks for the phone. Dispatch uses `vendor.phone`.

**Consequences.** Resolves: product.md §15 vendor OPEN, L-07 vendor-phone OPEN. L06-SEG-FILTER "Unassigned" = `vendor_id is null`. L-07 gains a call icon beside the vendor † (C-5 "calling a vendor"); it does not offer to log the call.

### D-17 · `message` generalises REMINDER; what counts as sent; what the tenant sees

**Conflict.** product.md §15 REMINDER: level, sent on, how sent, call notes. S-01 is opened for rent reminders, vendor dispatch, tenant updates, renewal notices, settlement summaries and payment links; "Only 'Yes' writes the reminder to the timeline" — for non-rent messages nothing says what is written or where. L-05 logs calls "as a reminder record with method call". T03-LST-REPORTS shows "the landlord's latest update if any", but updates leave via WhatsApp.

**Why not both.** One rent-specific entity cannot record the other five kinds; a tenant page cannot show an update that was never stored.

**Decision.** `message`: `landlord_id`, `kind` (`rent_reminder | call | vendor_dispatch | tenant_update | renewal_notice | settlement_summary | payment_link`), `rent_entry_id`, `request_id`, `agreement_id`, `settlement_id` (nullable, by kind), `tenancy_id`, `vendor_id` (nullable), `level` (1–3, rent reminders only), `channel` (`whatsapp | copy | email | call`), `body` (the composer text at the moment of "Yes"; null for calls), `call_outcome` and `call_note` (calls only), `confirmed_at`. A row is written only when the landlord answers "Yes" to "Did you send it?" (every channel, including S01-BTN-EMAIL) or saves L05-FRM-LOGCALL. "Not yet" returns to the composer with the draft intact. Cancel writes nothing. The ERD's REMINDER = `message` rows with kind `rent_reminder` or `call`.

T-03's "latest update" is the `body` of the most recent confirmed `tenant_update` message for that request, nothing else. Vendor dispatches, internal notes and rent messages are never readable by the tenant side.

**Consequences.** Resolves: product.md §15 REMINDER-parent OPEN (parent is the rent entry; calls hang off it too), S-01 non-rent timeline OPEN, S-01 "Not yet" OPEN, S-01 email-confirmation OPEN, S-01 recipient OPEN (tenancy phone or vendor phone by kind), T-03 latest-update OPEN, cross-cutting E1-02 OPEN (the toast follows the write), S-01 OPENED-FROM OPEN (S-02, L-06 toast and L-11 added). Confirming a `vendor_dispatch` sets a New request to Assigned (D-32). The landlord can type a cost into a tenant update; the system's draft never includes one (L-07 rule) and the stored body is only shown back to the same tenant who already received it.

### D-18 · `settlement` and `deduction` entities; corrections; the statement text

**Conflict.** L-13 has deductions (description, reason, amount, photo), a balance, "Prepare settlement statement", Mark settled, and "Corrections require a new statement that references the first" with no control for it. product.md §11: the assistant "turns the deposit amount, the deductions and their reasons into a written statement". P-02's contents have no narrative, landlord identity or unit identity. product.md §15 has no entity for either.

**Why not both.** —

### D-21 · The assistant's category and urgency suggestions

### D-24 · Rent rows: when they are created and for whom

**Conflict.** product.md §10: "1st of the month — a rent row is created per unit". L-12: expired agreements stay until renewed or ended.

**Why not both.** Per-unit rows for vacant months contradict L-04.

**Decision.** On the 1st (Asia/Kolkata) a job creates one `rent_entry` for every tenancy that is `active` on the 1st, with `amount_due` = the rent of the agreement in force on the 1st (D-08) and `due_on` from D-09. A tenancy ended mid-month keeps that month's full row; the landlord adjusts it with L05-BTN-EDIT if agreed. Row creation is idempotent per (tenancy, month) so L04-BNR-ROWSMISSING's button and the job can both run safely. Rows already created are never altered by renewals.

**Consequences.** Resolves: L-04 pro-rata OPEN. The seed's SC-43 and §5 rules match this.

### D-25 · Editing a rent entry's amount

**Why not both.** —

**Decision.** `amount_due` can be edited while the entry is not Paid (D-10), including after a part payment; the new value must be ≥ the amount already received. ### The seven mandatory schema corrections, in full

Each correction above is also a decision (D-01, D-10, D-08, D-09, D-13, D-14, D-15). This section restates them in one place for whoever builds or reviews the schema: what the source said, why it cannot be right, what we chose, and what it costs. Where this section and the decision it cites differ, the decision governs.

#### ★1 · Two tokens: `unit.door_token` and `tenancy.tenant_token` (D-01, D-02)

**What the source said.** T-01: "one unguessable token per unit". product.md §15: the UNIT has "its own reporting link". product.md §4: "Every tenant gets their own unit link". T-01 states and E4-2: "A former tenant's link stops working when the tenancy ends". P-03 prints a QR for a door sticker.

**Why it cannot be right.** One token cannot both outlive a tenancy on a printed sticker and die with it for the former tenant. Whichever owner is chosen, one of E4-2 or P-03 breaks.

**What we chose.** Two tokens, both served at `/u/:token`. `unit.door_token` is created with the unit, lives until the landlord regenerates it, and reaches T-01 and T-02 only. `tenancy.tenant_token` is created with the tenancy, revoked at End tenancy, and reaches all five tenant pages. Lookup resolves to exactly one of the two.

**What it costs.** Two unique indexed columns and two code paths in `src/server/tenant`. The landlord must learn which link is which; L-09 carries helper text for it. A door token on a vacant unit shows the invalid-link message, which a prospective tenant scanning the sticker will find unhelpful. One extra boundary test, E4-8.

#### ★2 · `payment` is a child of `rent_entry` (D-10, D-11, D-12)

**What the source said.** product.md §15 RENT ENTRY holds one "amount paid · paid on · payment reference · receipt number". L05-SEC-AMOUNT shows a single paid amount. L-04: "Marking paid twice is impossible".

**Why it cannot be right.** L-04 also says a part payment "remains in the Pending filter" and "a receipt is issued for the amount actually received". A second instalment needs a second date, reference and receipt number, and one set of fields cannot hold two.

**What we chose.** `rent_entry` is one row per tenancy per month carrying `amount_due` and `due_on`. `payment` is one row per amount received, with its own `receipt_number` and a `status` of `recorded | voided`. Paid, Due and Overdue are derived from the sum of recorded payments. Undo voids rather than deletes, so receipt numbers are never reused.

**What it costs.** Every rent query becomes an aggregate; status is computed, never stored, so there is no column to index for the Overdue filter. "Marking paid twice is impossible" is reworded: more than the balance is impossible. L-05 must list payments, including voided ones struck through, and gains a void action. Receipts are rendered on demand rather than filed.

#### ★3 · `agreement` is its own entity (D-08)

**What the source said.** product.md §15 TENANT carries "agreement start, end". L12-BTN-RENEW: "On save the agreement dates update". L11-SEC-AGREEMENT shows one start, end and rent.

**Why it cannot be right.** L-12 also says rent "changes take effect from the new start date" and "Rows already generated for earlier months are not altered". L-09's Rent tab shows "every month, every tenant, ever" at the amount that applied. The seed has three terms for one tenancy. Overwriting the dates on the tenancy destroys the history those screens display.

**What we chose.** `agreement` rows belong to a tenancy: `starts_on`, `ends_on`, `rent`, `rent_due_day`, `escalation_pct_applied`. Terms are non-overlapping and ordered. Record renewal inserts; it never edits. The agreement in force on a date is the one containing it, or the last one once it has expired.

**What it costs.** Every rent row carries an `agreement_id`, and row creation must find the agreement in force rather than read one column. L-11 shows a current term and a list of earlier ones, which the source did not draw. L-12's "the row leaves the list" needs explaining: it reappears under All with the new end date. Deleting a tenancy now cascades through agreements.

#### ★4 · `agreement.rent_due_day` (D-09)

**What the source said.** product.md §15 RENT ENTRY has a "due date" and no entity carries a due day. product.md §10 begins "1st of the month — a rent row is created", then asks "Paid by the due date?", then escalates at "Day 3 · 10 · 20". L04-CHP-STATUS shows "n days late".

**Why it cannot be right.** A due date with no stored due day has no source. "Day 3" cannot mean the 3rd of the month when the due date is the 5th, and the landlord-editable ladder in L-15 makes no sense counted from the 1st.

**What we chose.** `agreement.rent_due_day` (1–31), set per agreement so it can change at renewal. `rent_entry.due_on` is that day in the month, clamped to the month's last day. Days late, the ladder steps and "past due" are all measured from `due_on`.

**What it costs.** One more required field in the tenant form, defaulting to the 1st. Clamping needs tests for 29, 30 and 31. Pending and Overdue now depend on today's date, so the same month renders differently on different days. A due day changed at renewal gives L-09's history two different due dates.

#### ★5 · Notice belongs on `tenancy` (D-13)

**What the source said.** product.md §15 TENANT has "notice status". L08-CHP-STATUS shows "Notice period", L10-SEG-FILTER offers "On notice", L11-SEC-AGREEMENT displays "notice status". product.md M-7 and journey stage 5 say notice is recorded or given.

**Why it cannot be right.** Three screens display notice and none sets it; a "status" with no writer is always empty. The source also puts it on TENANT, which is the person, when notice ends one occupation of one unit.

**What we chose.** `tenancy.notice_given_on` and `tenancy.notice_vacate_on`, both nullable. On notice means an active tenancy with a vacate date. The landlord records it with a two-field form on L-11 (default vacate date today + 30 days, a form default only). Recording notice writes an audit event and changes nothing else: the agreement stands, rent rows keep generating, End tenancy is still the act that ends it.

**What it costs.** One new landlord control the source did not draw, `L11-BTN-RECORDNOTICE`. The tenant cannot give notice in the app, so the record is only as current as the landlord's data entry. There is no notice-period rule, so nothing warns when the vacate date arrives; C-9's calendar entry stays out of v1. Two nullable date columns.

#### ★6 · `audit_event` is its own entity (D-14)

**What the source said.** product.md §15 has no entity for history. L05-BTN-EDIT: "Any edit is recorded on the timeline with the old and new value." L-06: drag and reopen each "writes a timeline entry". L07-TML-HISTORY: "Every status change, message sent and note added". L-13: "a new statement that references the first".

**Why it cannot be right.** Timelines cannot be rendered from data that was never stored. REMINDER covers sent messages only; nothing covers edits, status changes, notes, voids or token regeneration.

**What we chose.** One generic `audit_event` table: `actor` (landlord, tenant, system), `entity_type`, `entity_id`, `kind`, `old_value` and `new_value` as JSON, `note`, `created_at`. Written on every mutation that a timeline shows. Internal notes are audit rows with `kind = note`. Timelines are the entity's audit rows merged with its confirmed messages.

**What it costs.** Every write path a timeline shows must log in the same transaction, or the timeline lies by omission; that discipline is the real cost. JSON values are not queryable and need per-kind rendering. The tenant side reads `status_change` rows only, which makes `kind` a security-relevant column under E4-4. The table is never pruned in v1.

#### ★7 · `document.is_protected` and document ownership (D-15)

**What the source said.** product.md §15 DOCUMENT "belongs to" a UNIT. L-13: once settled, "the statement becomes read-only" and "is filed under documents". L14-TBL-DOCS offers delete on every file.

**Why it cannot be right.** A file that any row of L-14 can delete is not read-only. A unit-only parent cannot hold a request's before photo, a tenancy's move-in photo, a deduction's photo or the landlord's logo, all of which the screens upload.

**What we chose.** `document` carries nullable `unit_id`, `tenancy_id`, `request_id`, `settlement_id` and `deduction_id`, a `type` enum, `uploaded_by` and `is_protected` (default false). Mark settled sets `is_protected` on the filed statement; L-14 disables delete for it and the server refuses regardless. Files live in a private bucket behind short-lived signed URLs. Tenant pages never fetch files; they show photo counts.

**What it costs.** Five foreign keys where the source drew one, plus a check that at least one owner is set. Protected rows cannot be removed even by the landlord who uploaded them; a wrong settlement is superseded, not deleted (D-18). Tenants lose photo thumbnails on T-02 and T-03.
